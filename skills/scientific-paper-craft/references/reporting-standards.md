# Select reporting standards by research design

For whole-paper drafting or submission-readiness assessment, identify the actual study
design, discipline, article type and venue requirements. Verify the current
guideline and relevant extensions at an authoritative source. Record the
source, access date, version and reason it applies. Ask a focused question if
uncertainty about the design changes the applicable standard.

For focused prose edits, preserve established reporting conventions. Repeat
the standards audit only if the requested change materially affects study
reporting.

Examples to recognize, not a universal checklist:

| Research design | Starting point |
|---|---|
| Systematic review | [PRISMA](https://www.prisma-statement.org/prisma-2020); select the appropriate extension, such as for a scoping review, when relevant |
| Randomized clinical trial | [CONSORT](https://www.consort-spirit.org/published-statements); distinguish trial reports from protocols and check relevant extensions |
| Observational epidemiological study | [STROBE](https://www.strobe-statement.org/checklists/), including its cohort, case-control or cross-sectional scope |
| Clinical prediction-model development, evaluation or updating | [TRIPOD+AI](https://www.tripod-statement.org/), covering regression and machine-learning models; check extensions for the specific model/task |

For other designs, consult verified disciplinary guidance and supporting skills
appropriate to that design. A narrative related-work section is not automatically
a systematic review; an observational software benchmark is not automatically
a STROBE study; a nonclinical predictor is not automatically a TRIPOD+AI study.

Use applicable items to check reporting of design, sampling or selection,
outcomes, analysis, uncertainty, limitations, ethics and availability as relevant.
Document omissions or justified non-applicability. Distinguish mandatory venue
requirements from recommendations. Do not retrofit an unperformed procedure or
invent registration, approval, measurements or analyses to complete a checklist.
Reporting completeness does not establish sound design, causal identification,
clinical utility or independent validation. Where no established guideline
fits, explain that and assess transparency using the design's own standards.
