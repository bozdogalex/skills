# Venue study and authorship check

Apply this reference to whole-paper preparation, venue selection/change, or
readiness review. For a narrow edit, reuse current project notes and do only
the checks affected by that edit.

## Resolve requirements

Identify the exact journal/conference, track, article type, submission stage,
and review model. Use the venue explicitly selected by the user; otherwise
derive it from current project context and confirm ambiguity. If undecided,
ask whether to choose a venue or work provisionally on venue-independent prose.

Open the official author guide and linked ethics policies. Record URLs,
access date, and applicable scope. Check author information, CRediT where
requested, final author consent, anonymity, related publication, AI assistance,
data/code availability, length, abstract, references, and figure/table rules.
Separate requirements from recommendations and portal-only checks. A saved
guide is a dated fallback, not proof of current rules. Report inaccessible
pages honestly; ask for a copy when the missing rule matters.

Compare the byline, contribution statement, affiliations, corresponding author,
equal-contribution notes, and package metadata. Consistent strings do not prove
authorship eligibility or author consent. Attribute roles to this study rather
than copying clinical or analysis roles from a prior manuscript. Distinguish
paper authors from artifact creators. Flag unresolved approval or disclosure
instead of changing roles or contacting people automatically.

## Study ten comparable recent articles

Select ten recent research articles from the actual venue, preferably the last
24 months, matching article type and methodological neighborhood. Use public
titles, abstracts, and keywords from context to guide relevance. Verify dates
and avoid future issue dates being mistaken for publication dates. Do not use
editorials or unrelated article types just to reach ten; include reviews when
the target manuscript is itself a review. If necessary widen
the period and disclose why. Treat this as a purposive sample, not a systematic
review or evidence of journal-wide prevalence.

For each, record title, year, DOI/publisher URL, type, accessibility, numbered
outline, and useful writing/presentation observations. Read accessible full text
before claiming section lengths, abstract content, or declaration wording.
If full text is blocked, try a legitimate author/institutional version. Mark
remaining gaps and give the user exact links; do not infer the missing structure
from metadata. Report the verified count even if fewer than ten are available.

For length comparisons, record version/layout and counting method. Compare
main-body section shares, excluding references/back matter consistently; label
heading-to-heading page spans as approximate. Separate Results-only from
combined Evaluation or Results/Discussion. Count words when reliable extraction
is available, with captions/tables inclusion stated. Manuscript and typeset
pages are not directly interchangeable.

Use the sample to learn plausible proportions, explanatory depth, abstract
style, and placement of comparisons. It does not impose a fixed outline,
reference count, or figure quota. Judge each proposed change against this
paper's story and the official rules. Journal policy outranks observed practice.

Reuse a documented study for the same venue/type within the same revision
cycle. Refresh official requirements for submission readiness and refresh the
article sample when the venue, paper type, or research scope materially changes.
Avoid rerunning ten-paper research after routine wording edits.
